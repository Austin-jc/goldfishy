The diagnosis

A strong engine behind an inconsistent storefront

The background engine is great — dual-queue, diff-gated, one combined organize_note call, a live status footer. What breaks the feel is everything around the trigger and the result. Five structural seams.

1

Two execution models for the same class of action

Bulk actions split into two opposite behaviors — sitting in the same menu. Auto-arrange and find-similar plan in the background and toast a Review. But auto-title, summarize-missing, and retag-all block the UI on an await with a bare spinner, then a count toast. The second pattern directly violates the project's own locked convention: "AI work runs in the background, never blocking."

The contract

Five principles to unify around

Codified as the AI-surface contract (extends the existing "AI feature guidelines"). Every current and future AI surface conforms to all five.

One execution model

No AI action ever blocks on an await. Per-note results fade in; multi-note runs as a tracked background task and announces completion.

Proportional feedback

Nothing the AI does is invisible. Metadata earns a quiet toast; a move or rewrite earns a preview or Review. Strength scales with stakes.

One home per capability

Per-note AI is summoned from the note; library AI from the sidebar; the palette mirrors it. No duplicate launchers, no diverging surfacing.

Marked & reversible

Keep the sage ✦ language; extend it to AI titles. Accept/dismiss controls are always visible, never hover-gated.

Legible

A running log of what AI did, where you already look. And a one-line reason when there's no AI — Manual mode or no backend.

The work

Four directions

Lowest-risk cleanup first (A), then net-new discoverable surface (B), consolidation (C), and the trust layer (D).

A

The consistency pass

Mostly aligns existing behavior to patterns already in the codebase. Highest leverage, lowest risk.

  • →Convert the three blocking sweeps to background tasks. aiTitleUntitled / aiSummarizeMissing / aiRetagAll adopt the same store-driven flow as startAutoArrange; progress shows in the existing queue footer; a completion toast replaces the frozen spinner.
  • →Give "Organize" feedback, on the right path. Point it at the same organize_note the queue uses, then toast "Added 3 tags · suggested folder: Recipes." Never silent again.
  • →Always-visible accept/dismiss. Drop the opacity-0 group-hover gate on proposed action items — controls show at rest.
  • →Fix the naming collision. "Tidy board" → Re-tidy; "Tidy up similar notes" → Merge duplicates (names the actual, higher-stakes action).
B

Inline editor AI

Today AI lives only in the editor header. The / slash menu and the selection bubble — where note apps universally expose inline AI — have none. Adding it is the biggest discoverability win, and it fits the existing surfaces with no new chrome. On-demand only: no ghost-text, nothing watching you type.

C

Consolidate entry points

One canonical primary surface per capability — per-note from the note, library-wide from the sidebar (shown contextually when relevant), the palette as the keyboard mirror. Duplicate launchers go; a modal's own re-run stays (it's iteration, not a duplicate).

Boundaries

What this keeps, and what it refuses

Every change either reduces interruption or holds it constant. Inherited from the project's consent ladder and AI feature guidelines.

Guard rails held

  • Consent ladder — silent only for additive, reversible metadata; moves & rewrites keep their gate.
  • Local by default — the automatic pipeline only ever uses the local backend.
  • AI must be ignorable — seamless never means louder.
  • Never blocking — background after typing stops; results fade in.

Deliberate non-goals

  • No new AI capabilities — this is a surface pass.
  • No ghost-text / as-you-type AI in the editor.
  • No new top-level AI panel — reuse editor, sidebar, palette, queue popover.
  • No change to the background engine beyond the manual-path redirect.
